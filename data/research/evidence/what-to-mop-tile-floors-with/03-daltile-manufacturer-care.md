# Daltile — Porcelain Tile Care & Maintenance (verbatim extracts)

**Fetched:** 2026-04-21

## Routine cleaner recommendation

> "Regular, basic cleaning with warm water and mild soap is sufficient to keep floors looking good, or you can use gentle, everyday multipurpose spray cleaner to remove soap scum, hard water deposits, and mildew."

## Dry-first rule

> "Sweep or vacuum tile prior to cleaning to remove dust or debris."

> "Sweep up or vacuum away loose dirt prior to mopping. Squeegee, wipe, or vacuum the floor dry after rinsing."

## What to avoid

> "Do not use cleaning products that contain acids or ammonia (and other harsh chemicals) as these can damage grout and glazed surfaces of the tile."

> "Choose products that are compatible with your grout to avoid damage or discoloration of the grout."

> "Cleaners that contain fluorine compounds will damage ceramic, glass and stone."

> "Acid-based or solvent-based chemicals should be avoided on fully polished impervious or polished glaze porcelain tile."

## After cleaning

> "Always dry your porcelain thoroughly after cleaning."

## Sealing

> "Porcelain does not require sealing, however, it is a good idea to seal grout or any joints."

## Unglazed tile

> "Unglazed tile should be cleaned routinely with concentrated tile cleaners that have a neutral pH, which are better suited for removing grease, oils and normal spills from unglazed products."

## Cleaner category preference

> "Use a nonhazardous and low VOC cleaning chemical."

> "Common household cleaners may be used within the guidance of this guide."

## Commercial/heavy-duty dwell

> "For heavy-soiled areas: allow 3-5 minutes dwell time. For commercial spaces: weekly cleaning in commercial spaces may use autoscrub machines with 5-10 minutes dwell time depending on the cleaning agent."

## Polished-tile caveat

> "Special care should be considered on Polished Tiles, as they will be more prompt to fine scratches."

## How TCR uses this source

- Daltile is the warranty authority for the majority of ceramic and porcelain tile installed in US residential in the last two decades.
- The "no acids, no ammonia" rule is the manufacturer-level line that TCR applies to the tile-face + grout-as-one-system.
- Vinegar-water recommendations from Bob Vila + Family Handyman are editorial minority positions that contradict the manufacturer; TCR acknowledges the divergence inline but sides with Daltile.
- The grout-damage angle is why: even though the glazed porcelain tile face itself is largely acid-tolerant, the cement-grout between tiles is not. Manufacturer draws the line at the system level.
- The "sweep/vacuum first + dry after" rule is near-universal across the manufacturer + editorial sources consulted.
- The "polished porcelain = extra caution" note underpins the glossy-vs-matte distinction on the page.
