# Natural Stone Institute — Cleaning Products for Natural Stone (verbatim extracts)

**URL:** https://www.naturalstoneinstitute.org/consumers/care/
**Fetched:** 2026-04-21
**Tier:** tier_primary (trade body for the US natural-stone industry; authoritative on stone care)

---

## Cleaner recommendation

> "Clean stone surfaces with a neutral cleaner, stone soap, or a mild liquid dishwashing detergent and warm water. You must have a cleaner that has the right pH level, which is seven (neutral)."

## Why vinegar and lemon juice are not acceptable

> "Products containing lemon, vinegar or other acids may dull or etch calcareous stones."

> "Calcium carbonate and calcite-based stones (marble and limestone primarily) will react to relatively mild acids, such as lemon juice, vinegar, and sliced tomatoes."

## Calcareous vs. siliceous distinction

> "Calcareous stone is composed mainly of calcium carbonate. Calcareous stones are sensitive to acidic solutions so mild, non-acidic cleaners are recommended."

> "Siliceous stone is one composed primarily of silicates. Siliceous stones are generally resistant to most acids found in kitchen settings, although acidic cleaners are still not recommended."

Calcareous: marble, travertine, limestone, onyx, some slate.
Siliceous: granite, quartzite, sandstone, most slate.

## Daily routine

> "Dust mop interior floors frequently using a clean non-treated dry dust mop. Sand, dirt and grit are abrasive and can damage natural stone."

## After wet cleaning

> "Use a clean rag mop on floors and a soft cloth for other surfaces, then rinse the surface thoroughly after washing with the soap solution and dry with a soft cloth. An excessive concentration of cleaner or soap may leave a film and cause streaks."

## What else to avoid

> "Scouring powders or creams often contain abrasives that may scratch certain stones."

> "Do not mix ammonia and bleach, as this combination creates a toxic and lethal gas."

## Hydrofluoric-acid warning (applies across all stone)

> "Many commercially available rust removers (laundry rust stain removers, toilet bowl cleaners) contain trace levels of hydrofluoric acid (HF). HF attacks silicates in addition to other minerals. All stones, including granite and quartzite, will be attacked if exposed to HF."
