## The honest answer (triangulated across 5 tier1 labs + 1 tier3 community source)

**No. Not in 2026.** Four of the five most-rigorous independent robot-vacuum testing shops no longer put any Roomba in their top 4 picks. The one remaining exception is CNET, who still recommend the new Roomba 205 DustCompactor Combo specifically for "best value + best hardwood under $500" (released under the post-bankruptcy Picea ownership).

The category has shifted to Roborock, Dreame, MOVA, Ecovacs, Eufy, and Narwal. Across RTINGS, Wirecutter, Vacuum Wars, and Modern Castle, the convergence is strongest on Roborock (top pick at RTINGS + Vacuum Wars; top pick at Wirecutter too with the Q7 M5+).

### Why the dominance flipped

Three forces converged over 2023-2025:

1. **Performance.** Wirecutter: "Roomba models have not performed well in our testing in recent years." The Eufy RoboVac 11S Max out-cleaned the Roomba 694 by 2-3× in Wirecutter's debris-pickup test. The Roomba j9+ couldn't reliably empty itself in Wirecutter's Cheerios + rice tests. Meanwhile, Roborock's lidar navigation, Dreame's obstacle-avoidance, and Eufy's price-to-performance ratio all pulled ahead.

2. **Company distress.** iRobot filed for bankruptcy in December 2025 (Wirecutter source). RTINGS removed the j7+ from their notable mentions in November 2025 citing "concerns about the long-term viability of the company." iRobot was subsequently acquired by Picea, its manufacturing partner (CNET source). CNET reports iRobot has assured existing product lines will continue to be supported, but the acquisition is recent and unsteady.

3. **Customer-service rot.** Wirecutter: "complaints about iRobot's customer service are rampant, reflecting a broader issue with its app and product support." This is the reputation-drag factor — a category that used to be trust-by-brand-name now requires picking around the brand.

## The narrow case where a Roomba is still the right buy

**One model, one use case, one buyer profile.** The 2026 Roomba 205 DustCompactor Combo (under $500) is CNET's "best value + best hardwood" pick. This is the post-bankruptcy, Picea-era release — it's not the same engineering lineage as the aging S9+ or discontinued j7+.

The 205 DustCompactor wins when:
- Budget cap is under $500 (eliminates the Roborock Saros 10R + Dreame X50 Ultra tier).
- Primary surface is hardwood (CNET: "earning the best score on hardwood floors").
- Reader wants a US-available brand with the broadest parts/service network, bankruptcy risk notwithstanding.
- Reader is not chasing top-tier obstacle avoidance, lidar mapping, or mop integration (those belong to Roborock + Dreame).
