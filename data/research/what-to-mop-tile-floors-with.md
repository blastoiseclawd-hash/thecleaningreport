# Research — /what-to-mop-tile-floors-with

## Angle

A tile-specific deep-dive that routes ceramic, glazed porcelain, polished porcelain, unglazed porcelain, and the natural-stone family (marble, travertine, limestone, granite, slate) to their manufacturer-warranty-approved cleaners. Resolves the vinegar-on-tile editorial-vs-manufacturer divergence inline.

## Competitor-vs-us diff

| Competitor treatment | Ours |
|---|---|
| Bob Vila + Family Handyman allow vinegar-water on porcelain and ceramic at 1/4 to 1 cup per gallon | Manufacturer line (Daltile, American Olean) is no acids, no ammonia — the grout is the weak point, not the tile face. We cite the manufacturer position and acknowledge the editorial divergence inline, not bury it. |
| Most tile-cleaning guides treat tile as one category | We split ceramic / glazed porcelain / polished porcelain / unglazed porcelain / natural stone (and within stone, calcareous vs siliceous) — each needs a different cleaner or technique. |
| Most guides skip the polished-vs-matte distinction | Daltile calls out polished porcelain specifically for fine-scratch risk; we carry that forward. |
| Most guides do not explain WHY vinegar is off-limits | We cite the Natural Stone Institute calcareous-stone etching mechanism + the cement-grout acid-dissolution angle for tile. |

## Final 5-7

**KEEP (featured in page):**

1. **Bona Stone, Tile & Laminate Floor Cleaner (128oz refill, ~$19)** — routine pH-neutral default for sealed ceramic, glazed porcelain, sealed polished porcelain, and luxury-vinyl. Widest surface compatibility of the pH-neutral family. Named in Bob Vila's testing. Manufacturer-stated for sealed tile, stone, laminate, and LVT. Cut candidate is Bona Hard-Surface 32oz — same chemistry, smaller bottle, better only for buyers without a spray mop.
2. **Black Diamond Stoneworks Marble & Tile (~$20 for 32 oz)** — natural-stone default (marble, travertine, limestone). pH-neutral, stone-specific branding. Bob Vila's Best for Natural Stone. Good fit for buyers who want a stone-specific rather than general-purpose cleaner.
3. **StoneTech All-Purpose Daily Cleaner (~$14 for 24 oz)** — secondary natural-stone pick. Laticrete/tile-industry parent company. Slightly wider Home Depot availability than Black Diamond.
4. **Granite Gold Daily Cleaner (~$10 for 24 oz)** — budget natural-stone pick. Grocery-store availability. pH-neutral. Note: some owners report residue at floor-dilution; fine for granite, marginal on polished marble.
5. **Method Squirt+Mop (~$6 for 25 oz)** — plant-based budget alternative on sealed ceramic and glazed porcelain. Not for natural stone. Under $6 retail; eco-brand discovery path.
6. **Daltile warm-water-and-mild-soap rule** — for tile owners who want the simplest (and most manufacturer-conservative) routine: warm water + mild pH-neutral dish soap as a dilute solution is the baseline. No named brand required.

**CUT:**

- **Aunt Fannie's Vinegar Floor Cleaner** — vinegar-based disqualifies on all natural stone and conflicts with Daltile/American Olean manufacturer position on tile grout. Naming it as a pick would require a "but not on these surfaces" asterisk longer than the pick itself.
- **Sheiner's Floor Cleaner Concentrate** — eco-friendly + cost-effective, but not specifically tile-positioned and not named by a manufacturer. Mentioned in Family Handyman roundup but not trust-signaled as tile-specific. Keep as category note, not featured pick.
- **Bissell SpinWave / Casabella / Swiffer WetJet** — tools not cleaners. This is a cleaner routing page. Commercial mop ranking lives on `/best-mop`.
- **Armstrong Once 'N Done** — mentioned by Family Handyman as eco-friendly but not widely available on Amazon as of 2026-04. Skip.
- **Windex / Pine-Sol / Fabuloso** — generic all-purpose; inappropriate for sealed flooring. Goes on the do-not-use list, not the pick list.

## Triangulation on contested claims

### Claim: Vinegar-water is an acceptable routine cleaner on porcelain and ceramic tile

- **Bob Vila** (tier_editorial): yes, 1/4 cup white vinegar per 2 gallons water monthly on porcelain.
- **Family Handyman** (tier_editorial): yes, 1 cup per gallon as a natural option on tile generally.
- **Daltile manufacturer** (tier_primary): no. "Do not use cleaning products that contain acids or ammonia (and other harsh chemicals) as these can damage grout and glazed surfaces of the tile."
- **American Olean manufacturer** (tier_primary): no. Explicitly calls out "vinegar and water" as a thing to avoid on natural stone, with related warnings on grout.
- **Resolution on the page:** manufacturer position leads. Acknowledge the editorial minority position inline ("Some DIY guides suggest a diluted vinegar rinse works on porcelain face, and on glazed ceramic that is largely true — the problem is not the tile, it is the cement grout between tiles, which is mildly acid-soluble and degrades over repeated exposure. The manufacturer draws the line at the system level."). Do NOT recommend vinegar-water as a routine tile cleaner.

### Claim: pH-neutral is required on natural stone

- **Natural Stone Institute** (tier_primary): yes, pH 7 neutral. Calcareous stones etch from any acid.
- **American Olean** (tier_primary): yes, no acid or bleach.
- **Bob Vila tile cleaner roundup** (tier_editorial): yes, "when cleaning natural stone tile, avoid cleaners with acidic ingredients because they can damage it."
- **Family Handyman tile-cleaning article** (tier_editorial): yes, "marble floors should avoid acidic cleaners like vinegar."
- **Resolution:** universal agreement. pH-neutral rule is the page's strongest claim, no divergence to resolve.

### Claim: Steam mops on sealed glazed tile are OK

- **Bob Vila** (tier_editorial): yes, "for ceramic or porcelain tile floors, steam cleaners are a smart option for deep cleaning and sanitation."
- **Natural Stone Institute** (tier_primary): no explicit position on steam but the moisture-tolerance guidance implies caution on porous stone.
- **Most tile manufacturers:** sealed glazed ceramic and sealed porcelain tolerate steam at low-moderate settings; unglazed porcelain and natural stone should not be steamed (moisture penetration and, on polished stone, thermal-shock risk).
- **Resolution on the page:** on sealed glazed ceramic and porcelain, steam is acceptable. On natural stone and unglazed porcelain, skip steam and use pH-neutral wet-mopping. Link to `/best-steam-mop` for readers whose tile is in the approved set.
