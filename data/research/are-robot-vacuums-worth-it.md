## Angle + narrative framework

**The Labor Math question in one line:** robot vacuum is worth it when (hours reclaimed per week × your effective hourly wage × 52 weeks × expected lifespan in years) > (purchase price + maintenance + consumables over that lifespan). For Labor Traders with $40+/hour effective wage and multi-floor homes with pets, this breaks in the robot's favor by year two. For hardwood apartments with $15/hour effective wage and minimal foot traffic, this never breaks in favor — a $200 cordless stick wins.

**Where the math breaks (do-not-buy cases):**
1. Studio or small 1-bedroom hardwood: total cleaning time is already under 15 min/week; break-even never arrives.
2. Heavy shag carpet: robots cannot groom pile-lifted carpet effectively; tier-1 labs document the performance gap.
3. Cluttered floors with cords, toys, low furniture <3-inch clearance: navigation fails weekly, reader ends up moving clutter for the robot and losing the time-save.
4. Pet-mess households without real-time response: robot drags a fresh pet accident across the whole floor; the "labor saved" becomes "floor rewashed."
5. Multi-level homes without stair-aware hardware: the robot does one floor; reader still needs another vacuum for the rest.
6. Dark floors on cheaper models: cliff sensors mis-read dark rugs as stairs and the robot refuses to enter.

**Break-even table by household type (for page body):**
