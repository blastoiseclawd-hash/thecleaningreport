## Scope

TheCleaningReport should be treated as a broad indoor home-cleaning brand, not a pure vacuum site.

That said, the buyer evidence is not evenly distributed across the niche yet. The strongest demand, the cleanest intent, and the clearest monetization all sit inside floor care:

- robot vacuums
- cordless vacuums
- vacuums for pet hair
- steam mops

This research is meant to define the real buyer set for that launch spine first, while still giving the brand room to expand into broader indoor-cleaning tools later.

## Research Synthesis

### What mainstream cleaning buyers actually optimize for

The American Cleaning Institute's 2025 Spring Cleaning Survey is useful because it grounds this niche in ordinary households, not hobbyists:

- `80%` of U.S. households engage in some form of spring cleaning at least once a year
- among spring cleaners, `39%` say effectiveness is the most important quality in choosing cleaning products
- `18%` put cost first
- `13%` prioritize a product being multipurpose

The same survey also shows that people connect cleaning to well-being, but still buy on practical criteria first. That supports a shopper-first, anti-hype brand posture for TheCleaningReport.

### What vacuum buyers care about once they get specific

The ACSI 2025 vacuum-cleaner benchmarks are useful because they show which product traits actually drive satisfaction:

- ease of operation
- app quality and app reliability
- ease of emptying the canister or dust collector
- durability
- ease of cleaning the vacuum itself
- carpet and hard-floor effectiveness
- noise during operation

That lines up almost perfectly with the search behavior in the Ahrefs exports. Buyers are not just asking for "best"; they are asking for:

- under specific budgets
- for specific floors
- for pet hair
- for apartments
- for quiet use
- for self-emptying convenience
- for no-tangle or easy-clean maintenance

### How buyers research before they buy

The search patterns show a consistent three-step research behavior:

1. broad comparison query
   Examples:
   - `best robot vacuum`
   - `best cordless vacuum`
   - `best vacuum for pet hair`
   - `best steam mop`

2. use-case or budget refinement
   Examples:
   - `best robot vacuum under $500`
   - `best cordless vacuum under $200`
   - `best vacuum for pet hair on furniture`
   - `best steam mop for hardwood floors`

3. trust-checking through outside voices
   Examples:
   - `best robot vacuum reddit`
   - `best robot vacuum reviews youtube`
   - `best cordless vacuum reddit`
   - `best cordless vacuum under $100 consumer reports`
   - `best vacuum for pet hair rtings`
   - `best steam mop reddit`

So the site should assume buyers are comparison-shopping in Google first, then validating in communities, expert-review ecosystems, and video demos before they commit.

## Final Avatar Set

## 1. Automation-Minded Floor-Care Upgrader

- Demographics and context:
  Busy households, often multi-room homes with mixed floors, who are willing to spend more if a cleaning tool reliably removes recurring work from the week.
- Relationship to the category:
  This is the core robot-vacuum buyer. They are not vacuum enthusiasts; they are outsourcing a chore.
- What they search:
  - `best robot vacuum`
  - `best robot vacuum under $500`
  - `best self emptying robot vacuum`
  - `best robot vacuum obstacle avoidance`
  - `best robot vacuum for pet hair`
  - `best robot vacuum reddit`
  - `best robot vacuum reviews youtube`
- Budget range:
  Usually `~$300 to $900`, with strong budget refinement around `under $500`, `under $600`, and `under $1000`.
- Decision factors:
  Ease of operation, dock upkeep, app reliability, self-emptying value, obstacle handling, pet-hair performance, carpet vs hard-floor results, and noise.
- What they do not care about:
  Fancy lifestyle branding, oversized accessory bundles, or spec-sheet bragging that does not reduce daily work.
- How they research:
  Google shortlist first, then Reddit and YouTube to sanity-check navigation quality, tangles, dock behavior, and whether the app is flaky.
- Where they spend attention:
  Best-of roundups, Reddit threads, video demos, and update-year queries like `2025` or `2026`.
- Tone and page structure that works:
  BLUF upfront, clear tiering, a maintenance snapshot, and fast answers about when auto-empty and app features are worth paying for.

## 2. Practical Cordless Buyer

- Demographics and context:
  Mainstream everyday cleaner, often in a small-to-mid-size home or apartment, who wants fast pickup and manageable storage without paying premium prices for features they will not use.
- Relationship to the category:
  This is the mainstream high-volume buyer in the niche. They want a better daily vacuum, not a whole cleaning system.
- What they search:
  - `best cordless vacuum`
  - `best budget cordless vacuum`
  - `best cordless vacuum under $200`
  - `best cordless vacuum under $300`
  - `best lightweight cordless vacuum`
  - `best cordless vacuum with removable battery`
  - `best cordless vacuum small apartment`
  - `reddit best cordless vacuum`
- Budget range:
  Usually `~$100 to $300`, with occasional stretch to `~$500` when battery life or suction feels worth it.
- Decision factors:
  Price-to-performance, suction that feels real on normal floors, battery life, weight, easy emptying, storage footprint, and ease of maintenance.
- What they do not care about:
  Premium app ecosystems, aspirational design language, or specialized features that complicate ownership.
- How they research:
  Broad best-of queries, budget queries, retailer-availability thinking, and community checks to avoid buying weak cordless models that lose suction quickly.
- Where they spend attention:
  Google comparisons, budget roundups, Reddit threads, and Consumer Reports-style trust checks.
- Tone and page structure that works:
  Direct, concise, shortlist-heavy, with clear "best under $X" guidance and strong notes on runtime, weight, and storage.

## 3. Pet-Mess Floor Manager

- Demographics and context:
  Higher-friction homes with dogs or cats, heavier shedding, more upholstery or stairs to maintain, and very low tolerance for vacuums that clog, wrap, or underperform on mixed surfaces.
- Relationship to the category:
  This buyer is not just shopping for "a vacuum"; they are shopping against recurring frustration.
- What they search:
  - `best vacuum for pet hair`
  - `best vacuum for pet hair on carpet`
  - `best vacuum for pet hair on furniture`
  - `best vacuum for pet hair and litter`
  - `best vacuum for pet hair no tangle`
  - `best vacuum for pet hair easy to clean`
  - `best robot vacuum for pet hair under $500`
  - `best vacuum for pet hair reddit`
- Budget range:
  Usually `~$200 to $700`, depending on whether they end up with a cordless, upright, canister, or robot solution.
- Decision factors:
  Hair pickup, brush-roll tangles, upholstery tools, furniture/stairs usefulness, carpet performance, hard-floor transition, HEPA or sealed filtration interest, and how annoying the vacuum is to clean after every run.
- What they do not care about:
  Weak mop-combo gimmicks, sleek industrial design, or "smart" features that do not improve hair handling.
- How they research:
  They search problem-first, then go to Reddit or owner communities to find out which models clog, which ones wrap hair, and which tools actually help on furniture and stairs.
- Where they spend attention:
  Mixed-surface comparison pages, pet-owner threads, furniture and carpet pain-point discussions, and durability conversations.
- Tone and page structure that works:
  Problem-framed, maintenance-heavy, with explicit failure modes and very clear notes about tangle resistance, furniture performance, and real-life cleanup burden.

## 4. Surface-Specific Deep Cleaner

- Demographics and context:
  The shopper dealing with a specific floor or cleaning-job risk: hardwood, laminate, tile, grout, vinyl plank, or a periodic deep-clean need that feels different from everyday vacuuming.
- Relationship to the category:
  This is the steam-mop and specialty-cleaning buyer. They need confidence that the tool is appropriate before they need a big feature list.
- What they search:
  - `best steam mop`
  - `best steam mop for hardwood floors`
  - `best steam mop for laminate floors`
  - `best steam mop for tile floors`
  - `best steam mop for grout`
  - `is a steam mop better than a mop`
  - `best steam mop under $100`
  - `best steam mop reddit`
- Budget range:
  Usually `~$80 to $250`, with the strongest budget language clustered around `under $100` and `budget steam mop`.
- Decision factors:
  Surface safety, whether steam is appropriate at all, drying time, grout or tile payoff, refill hassle, attachments, and whether the result is actually better than simpler alternatives.
- What they do not care about:
  App features, luxury branding, or vague "deep clean" claims without surface-specific guidance.
- How they research:
  Search starts with a floor-type question or a safety concern, then moves to communities and cautionary threads to avoid damaging hardwood or wasting money on the wrong tool.
- Where they spend attention:
  Use-case pages, floor-type comparisons, Reddit threads, and targeted review pages that explain when to use steam and when not to.
- Tone and page structure that works:
  Caution-first, matrix-driven, with clear use-this / avoid-this guidance and fast answers about surface compatibility.

## Validation Notes

### Why the original seed set changed

The seed set was directionally right, but one part needed revision:

- `Small-home or apartment cleaner` is real, but it is not a strong enough standalone top-level avatar for this site yet.

The keyword evidence shows apartment, lightweight, quiet, removable-battery, and small-job behavior clustering mostly inside the broader cordless-value lane. So the better move is:

- keep that context alive
- absorb it into `Practical Cordless Buyer`
- avoid forcing a weaker standalone avatar that would not justify its own desk or page system

### Why this set works

- `Practical Cordless Buyer` is the mainstream, high-volume avatar.
- `Automation-Minded Floor-Care Upgrader` captures the higher-AOV launch opportunity.
- `Pet-Mess Floor Manager` creates clearly different content choices and comparisons.
- `Surface-Specific Deep Cleaner` justifies a separate caution-first content style and future maintenance-tool expansion.

Each segment searches differently enough to justify different pages, page structures, and future desk voices.

## Navigation and Roadmap Implications

### What should lead the first content wave

The first three launch pages still make sense:

1. `best-robot-vacuum`
2. `best-cordless-vacuum`
3. `best-vacuum-for-pet-hair`

Why:
- they map cleanly to the first three strongest avatars
- they reflect the strongest validated demand and monetization lanes
- they let the site sound broad without pretending the cleaning-tool cluster is equally ready on day one

### What should follow soon after

The next high-value support or spoke families should come from the research behavior inside these avatars:

- robot vacuum budget pages
- robot vacuum self-empty and obstacle-avoidance pages
- cordless vacuum under-$X pages
- pet-hair pages for carpet, furniture, stairs, and no-tangle maintenance
- steam-mop pages by floor type and surface safety

### What should stay delayed

- generic cleaning-tool coverage that does not connect cleanly to a launch avatar
- maintenance-tool pages that still have weak evidence or weak monetization depth
- nav expansion into broader cleaning tools before the floor-care baseline is real
