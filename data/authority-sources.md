# TheCleaningReport — Authority Source Map

**Purpose.** Per-niche tier mapping for research docs. Every Phase 2 research session on a TCR cluster starts here. Sources below are the ones that produce information-gain-worthy data for *this* site's niches: robot vacuums, cordless/upright vacuums, steam mops, carpet cleaners, hardwood/laminate/stone care, chemical cleaning bundle.

**Portability note.** This doc is TCR-specific. Other sites (BCF coffee, BPP pets, CWP water) each get their own `authority-sources.md`. Do NOT copy TCR's list onto another niche — cleaning's tier1 (VW/MC/RTINGS) has no overlap with coffee's tier1 (Sweet Maria's / Home-Barista / Hoffmann), and water's tier1 is primary-source-dominated (NSF/EPA/EWG) rather than editorial.

**Last verified:** 2026-04-19 (Pass 1 of best-robot-vacuum rebuild).

### Paywalled sources — DO NOT cite

Per Lee's directive (2026-04-19), TCR research docs do not cite sites that paywall their data or editorial. Even if free snippets are visible, clicking through routes the reader to a subscription wall — bad UX and against the information-access commitment.

- **RTINGS** (rtings.com) — Numeric scores behind Insider subscription as of 2026-04. Do not cite, even the free editorial. Prior 2026-04-19 attempt removed from evidence folder.
- **Consumer Reports** (consumerreports.org) — Testing paywalled. Cite only if we hold a subscription + can quote from a screenshot we captured ourselves.
- **NYT Wirecutter** (nytimes.com/wirecutter, thewirecutter.com) — Paywalled. Use for divergence awareness only; never cite. If we're referencing "how our take differs from Wirecutter," paraphrase from memory without a URL.

### Cross-source divergence is information gain

During the 2026-04-19 best-robot-vacuum research, VW's 2026 top 20 was 80% Dreame/MOVA. MC's 2026 top 8 was 50% Roborock + 25% iRobot. CNET (replacement tier1 #3 as of 2026-04) publishes its own named picks that rarely match either. **No single product appears in all three top picks.** This divergence is more useful than consensus — it means each lab's methodology weights different traits, and our page's angle should surface WHY the labs disagree, not hide the disagreement behind a false consensus.

Rule: if a lab's ranking diverges from the others, capture the divergence as a structural note. Don't average it away.

### Community sentiment rule

If a product's owner sentiment at 6mo+ contradicts its tier1 lab ranking, document the contradiction. Example discovered during 2026-04-19 research: RTINGS ranks Roborock Saros 10R #1 overall, but notes "disappointing pet hair pickup on carpet" + "struggles to pull embedded fur from deep carpet fibers." That's a structural limitation the reader needs to know before buying, even though the lab ranking is #1. Surface it.

## Blocked / flagged (never cite)

Patterns to never add to tier1/tier2:

- **AI-generated review sites** (no named human byline, no about page, suspiciously uniform content, every review a "Best X for Y" listicle with Amazon affiliate links and zero testing).
- **Sub-DR-30 editorial sites** with no testing methodology disclosed — even if they SEO-rank.
- **Manufacturer-syndicated "reviews"** that read like press releases.
- **Listicle sites with unnamed "expert panels"** (no individual reviewer bylines).

When in doubt, check: does the site publish a "How we test" or "Methodology" page? Does it name the reviewer? Is there a physical lab or testing setup disclosed? If any is no, don't cite.

## Niche-specific notes

### Robot vacuum (Labor Math Desk flagship)
- Tier1 divergence is structural (see 2026-04 best-robot-vacuum research). Our angle: surface the divergence + frame for Labor Trader avatar (cost-per-hour-of-labor-saved).
- VW's methodology is the most transparent (accordion ratings with numeric averages). Default to VW for quantitative claims.
- MC's 13-test battery publishes exact percentages; great for claim substantiation.
- CNET / Tom's Guide fill the named-editorial tier1 slot now that RTINGS is paywalled. Neither publishes a standardized score matrix, so their contribution is qualitative editorial reasoning with a named tester — use for validating or disputing VW/MC picks.

### Carpet cleaner machine (Safe Surface Desk / Event-Driven Restorer)
- CRI Seal-of-Approval is the Safe Surface Desk anchor. Always cite when a product carries it.
- MC + GHI both test carpet cleaner machines with standardized stain batteries.
- VacuumLand has the deepest long-term owner data on Bissell / Rug Doctor / Hoover product lineage.

### Steam mop (Safe Surface Desk / Surface Protector)
- **Flooring manufacturer warranty voidance** is the #1 structural claim. Bona, Shaw, Armstrong, Mannington each have explicit "steam mop voids warranty" language. Primary-source cite every time.
- IICRC S100 (carpet/upholstery) does NOT cover hard-surface; don't use as hardwood/laminate anchor.

### Cordless / stick / upright vacuum (Everyday Cleaning + Labor Math overlap)
- VW's anemometer airflow test + 7-inch hair-tangle test (from Labor Math Desk trust lane) are the most citable quantitative tests.
- CR + GHI publish their own editorial winners; useful Tier2 validation.

### Mop / non-steam (Everyday Cleaning / Surface Protector backlog)
- Minimal tier1 testing here. Lean on flooring-manufacturer care guides + community sentiment. Commercial claims are weaker; set reader expectations.

### Chemical cleaning bundle (Safe Surface Desk)
- Primary-source-dominated: EPA Safer Choice, EWG, Made Safe. Editorial review sites (tier2) are noisy here.
- Never claim a product "disinfects" unless the EPA registration + kill-list is cited.

## Research-doc source requirements (summary)

A TCR research doc must cite:
- **≥2 of 3 tier1 methodology sources** for every product in the Final 5-7 (triangulation).
- **≥1 tier3 community thread** per Final 5-7 product (6mo+ sentiment).
- **≥1 primary source** for any hard-surface/chemistry/standards claim.

This doc's tier1/tier2/tier3 domains must all be present in the parent repo's `data/trusted-sources.json` allowlist. If a domain you want to cite isn't on the allowlist, either add it there (with Lee approval + proof of named-byline + disclosed-methodology) or drop the citation.
